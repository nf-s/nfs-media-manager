# Spotify Playback

Optional in-app playback of library albums and tracks through a connected Spotify Premium account. It is off until connected from the player bar. The Spotify logo on its own line in a detail header opens the service; music titles open library details. Play buttons remain visible when playback is disconnected, with a connection explanation.

When Spotify substitutes a market edition for a saved track, the player keeps the library link when Spotify confirms the substitution. For playback started elsewhere, the player can also open a saved edition when Spotify's recording code, album title, track title, duration and ordered artist credits identify a single library track. Artist names in the player open the artist inspector. A name without a resolved artist ID opens an in-app artist search.

## Turning it on

1. Configure Spotify and its redirect URI as described in [Spotify albums and CSV imports](spotify-album-imports.md#account-setup).
2. Choose **Connect Spotify** in the player bar at the bottom of the app. Spotify asks for consent, then returns to the app. The bar's prompt can be dismissed; **Player** in the sidebar foot brings it back.

Connecting requests the read-only [library and playlist scopes](spotify-album-imports.md#account-setup) plus the playback scopes `streaming`, `user-read-email`, `user-read-private`, `user-read-playback-state` and `user-modify-playback-state`, and `user-read-recently-played` for the account's play history. Playback works without the play history; it is used only to [move the queue past what Spotify played](#picking-the-queue-up-again) while the app was not following the device. A connection made before the play history was requested shows **Play history: Not allowed** under **Play on → Connections**; **Allow** repeats the consent with it. **Turn off playback**, under **Play on → Connections** in the player, reconnects without the playback scopes, keeping the library and playlist scopes and, if it was granted, [permission to change playlists](tracks-and-playlists.md#adding-an-album-to-a-playlist). Connecting from **Imports → Spotify** keeps whichever access is already granted.

Requirements:

- A Spotify Premium account. Playing in the browser and controlling other devices both need it.
- The Spotify developer app must allow the Web Playback SDK, and in development mode the account must be on the app's user list.
- A browser that supports protected media in a secure context (HTTPS or `localhost`). Other devices can still be controlled when the browser cannot play.

## Using it

- **Grid tiles** show a visible play button over the artwork. Album and track list/table views also show play controls, and album/playlist track tables use the same controls. Queue actions remain available when playback is connected.
- **The album drawer** has **Play** and **Add to queue** in its heading. In **Tracks**, a track number starts playback from that row using the table's search and sort order, and each row can queue the single track. Returning to this source reopens Tracks with those settings.
- **Playing from a view:** starting playback from a browse view or playlist keeps that view as the playback source, with its filters and sort order. Playback continues through the source even after navigating away or changing the visible view. Starting from another view replaces the source. Unmatched tracks are skipped.
- **The player dock** is always at the bottom once playback is connected: current track, shuffle, previous, play/pause, next, a seek bar, the queue, the device, volume and a link to the track in Spotify. Track, album and artist names open their stored details, including items outside the library. Opening a known Spotify album with missing details requests that album. The book beside a track or album adds that item to the library. On phones the dock is a mini-player above the tab bar that opens the player in the same drawer as details: it covers the mini-player and tab bar, and closes by swiping down from its header, Close, Escape or a tap outside.
- **Queue:** manually queued items play in their listed order after the current source track, then playback resumes from the source. A queued album plays in full. Entries can be played now, reordered, removed or cleared. **Back to source** returns to the saved view, and the queue shows up to ten upcoming source tracks below the manual queue, under **Next from**. The queue is saved for the account, so it is still there after a reload and in every other open copy of the app. It is the app's own queue, separate from Spotify's.
- **Next from:** each upcoming source track has the same row controls as a queued entry, and the two lists share one way of dragging: a line shows where the row will land, and it moves when it is dropped.
  - **Skip to** (the play button) starts that track now. The source tracks listed before it are passed over; the manual queue is kept and plays after it, then the source carries on.
  - **Move to queue** puts the track at the end of the manual queue; dragging it onto the queue puts it where it is dropped. The source then goes on without it.
  - The arrows and dragging reorder the upcoming tracks. The source is read in its own order again after the tracks that were moved.
  - **Remove** takes the track out of the source for as long as that source plays, also when shuffle is turned on afterwards.
  - A queued entry cannot be dragged into **Next from**, which lists the source's own tracks. Turning shuffle on or off draws the upcoming tracks again, so an order made by hand is not kept; tracks moved to the queue or removed stay out.
- **Queue order on Spotify Connect.** When playback starts, the device gets the manual queue followed by the upcoming source tracks. When you add a track during playback, the app checks Spotify’s queue. If Spotify can put it in the requested place, the app adds it to the device’s queue ahead of the source. The current track keeps its position, including while paused.
- **How far the device plays ahead.** A track can start with up to 50 following tracks, including up to 30 from the source. This list ends before a queued album or repeated track. Up to 50 manual tracks can be staged in Spotify’s queue during playback. The device can play what it has received while all pages are closed. The app saves the remaining order for when it rejoins.
- **What starts automatically.** An open page can start the next item when a device reaches a track displaced by an edit or finishes the list it received. It can recognise the end of the list when the device stops or continues into other music. If someone starts other music earlier, the page shows what is playing. If the device stops reporting or the page misses 90 seconds of playback, the app keeps the queue and source. **Play** or **Next** carries on.
- **Reloading and reopening:** the queue, the source, the device and the playing position are saved for the signed-in account, with no time limit. A page that is reloaded, or opened later on any computer or phone, picks them up. Music in a browser stops when its page closes or reloads, so there is nothing to rejoin there: the track is shown paused where it stopped, also after a reload of the page that was playing in **This browser**, and **Play** carries on from there.
- **Rejoining a device after every page was closed:** a Spotify device or [Sonos room](sonos.md#closing-every-page) keeps playing the tracks it was given. The first page opened afterwards reads the device and rejoins it when it still holds that queue further along: on the saved track at or after the saved position, or on a later track it was given. Nothing is sent to the device; the dock shows the track it has reached, the queue and source continue from there, and the controls work as before. The device is not rejoined when it plays or holds something else (another track, a playlist, an earlier track of the queue, or the saved track again from its start), or when it has stopped. Other music on the device, playing or left paused there, is then shown as playback started elsewhere, with the queue and source kept for whatever is played next; **Play** resumes paused music where it is. A list the device played to its end leaves the queue after those tracks. When nothing plays any more, the track is shown paused at the saved position and **Play** starts it there. Where the device is not rejoined, the queue is first [moved on past the tracks Spotify played meanwhile](#picking-the-queue-up-again). A line above the dock says **Rejoining the queue on …** while the device is read, then whether it was rejoined or why not. A successful rejoin message clears after ten seconds. **Dismiss** or any control clears the outcome sooner. A page that takes the session over from another open page to run a command follows the device as that page left it, without this check.
- **Several open copies:** every signed-in browser shows the same track, queue and source. One of them runs the queue, which means it gives the device the tracks due next; the queue panel on the others names it. Pause, resume, seek and volume work from any copy. Spotify controls go straight to the device. Sonos controls first make that copy the driver. Starting something, Next, Previous, a queue change, shuffle or **Play on** in another copy moves the running of the queue to that copy; the device keeps playing. To move the music to another copy, choose **This browser** under **Play on** in that copy. When the browser running the queue is closed, another visible copy takes over at once, and within 12 to 20 seconds when it drops out without closing. Music in **This browser** stops when that page closes; the other copies show the track paused where it stopped.
- **Shuffle** is remembered in the browser for track lists and playlists, and can be set before anything plays. It shuffles the remaining source without changing the current track or manually queued items; turning it off restores the remaining source order. An album, or a track played from an album or the album library, always starts in order; shuffle can be turned on for it, and that does not change the remembered setting.
- **Play on:** **This browser** or any device Spotify lists for the account, then the account's [Sonos rooms](sonos.md), which play through Sonos and are chosen the same way, and the Spotify and Sonos connections. Playback that another app moves to a different device is followed there as soon as this browser stops playing, so controls always go to the device that has the playback. When that device goes quiet, the device chosen here is the target again.
- **Playback started elsewhere:** when the account is playing on another device, for example from the Spotify app on a phone, the dock shows that track and controls that device: play/pause, previous, next, seek and volume. Playing an album here then plays it on that device; choose **This browser** to move it. Queued items do not start on their own after playback the app did not start: press **Next** or play one from the queue. Paused playback on another device is not picked up while the app is open, unless that device [has moved on from a track kept here](#picking-the-queue-up-again); a page opened on a saved session shows what its device holds, also paused.
- **Devices apps cannot start:** Spotify marks some speakers, TVs and receivers as restricted and accepts no commands for them. Playback there is shown with the transport, seek bar and volume disabled, unless a [Sonos connection](sonos.md) controls it; a Sonos room is then also started from here, through Sonos. Nothing new can be started on any other such device: playing an album or track, or a queue entry, is refused with a message and the device keeps its playback. It plays the tracks that were sent with the current one before the playback moved there; queue changes made after that reach it only once playback is started again on another device. **Play on** moves the playback to a device this app can start. A speaker that a Spotify app plays to over Spotify Connect is typically restricted.

Albums and tracks without a valid Spotify album URL or track ID show a disabled play button explaining that no recording is available.

## How it works

`POST /api/v1/spotify/playback/token` (authenticated, same-origin) returns the account's access token and its expiry. The server refreshes it through the Spotify request gate when it is within a minute of expiring. The refresh token, client secret and PKCE verifier never leave the server. The browser keeps the access token in memory only and asks again shortly before it expires. The endpoint answers 422 unless the stored account has every playback scope.

The browser then talks to Spotify directly:

- The Web Playback SDK script loads from `sdk.scdn.co` only when playback is connected. The browser registers as a Spotify device named **Media Manager** the first time it plays or is chosen as **This browser**, not when the page loads.
- Player commands and device listings go to `api.spotify.com/v1/me/player`. For an eligible Connect queue addition, the app reads the account queue with `GET /queue` and adds the track to the selected device with `POST /queue`. These calls come from the browser, so they do **not** pass through the server's Spotify request gate and are not recorded as provider requests. While something plays on another device, the browser reads the player state every three seconds so the queue can advance and the dock follows changes made elsewhere; playing in the browser reads state from the SDK without Web API polling. While nothing is playing, or playback is paused, a visible page reads the player state every five seconds to notice playback started or resumed elsewhere; a hidden page does not, and checks once when it is shown again. For five seconds after **Play on** moves playback, a report that still names the old device is ignored.

[`apps/web/utils/playback-engine.ts`](../apps/web/utils/playback-engine.ts) owns the queue, the device target and the session rules in [`playback.ts`](../apps/web/utils/playback.ts); it takes the Spotify API, token source and SDK loader as arguments so it runs under test without a browser. [`playback-provider.tsx`](../apps/web/components/playback-provider.tsx) exposes it to the shell, [`player-dock.tsx`](../apps/web/components/player-dock.tsx) and [`play-controls.tsx`](../apps/web/components/play-controls.tsx).

### The shared session

The server keeps one session record for each signed-in user: a revision number, the saved state, and a lease naming the page that runs the queue (the driver). `POST /api/v1/playback/session` (authenticated, same-origin) is the only exchange. A page sends an ID made for that load of the page, a name for its browser such as "Safari on iPhone", the revision it knows, and, when it is the driver and something changed, the state to save. The call:

- gives the caller the lease when it is free, already the caller's, or asked for with `claim`, and renews it for 12 seconds;
- saves the state when the caller holds the lease and its revision is the stored one, which adds one to the revision;
- returns the stored state when the caller's revision is not the stored one.

`POST /api/v1/playback/session/release` gives the lease up as a page closes, with a last save when it fits in the request a closing page may send.

A page asks every three seconds while something plays and every eight while nothing does. A page waiting for a room list checks the shared session every second. A hidden driver keeps its lease while running a playing queue. A page asks again when it becomes visible. A driver that stops asking lets its lease lapse, and the next page to ask becomes the driver. The driver saves 400 ms after a change and the playing position every ten seconds. A follower shows the shared queue. It can read Spotify playback directly. For Sonos it displays the driver’s room, playback and volume reports. Room menu requests from any page go through the driver and concurrent requests share one result. A queue command or Sonos control on a follower first asks for the lease with `claim`, restores the stored state and then runs. Browsers allow audio only from a click on the page, and that exchange outlasts the click, so **This browser** chosen on a follower unlocks its audio before the exchange.

The saved state holds the queue, the current item, the tracks the device was given and how far it is in them, the device, shuffle, the last reported position, and the source as its query, its progress and up to 50 upcoming tracks. On a Sonos room it also holds how much of the playing track has been heard, for [scrobbling](sonos.md#scrobbling-to-lastfm). A page that takes it over reads the device once. If the device is on one of the tracks it was given, the queue is advanced to that track and nothing is sent. The session records which tracks Spotify accepted into its queue ahead of the source. If a page closes while an addition is in flight, the session also keeps the possible new order. Seeing the added track on the device can confirm that Spotify received it. When either order could explain the track on the device, items that may still be due stay in the app queue. If the device plays something else, the queue and source are kept and that playback is shown. The source's entries are read again from the API as they are needed.

A tab keeps a second ID in its session storage, the same after a reload, so that a saved session playing in **This browser** can tell whether that browser is this tab. A tab that was the driver when it was reloaded asks for the lease back as it loads.

In the Cloudflare deployment the record is held by a `PlaybackSessionStore` Durable Object for each user (binding `PLAYBACK_SESSIONS`); the Node development server keeps it in memory. [`playback-sync.ts`](../apps/web/utils/playback-sync.ts) runs the exchange around the engine, and [`playback-session.ts`](../apps/server/src/services/playback-session.ts) holds the lease rules. If the endpoint cannot be reached, Spotify playback can continue locally. Sonos operations resume after the server grants a live lease. On reconnection, a Spotify queue started locally replaces the saved session when the page holds the lease.

### Picking the queue up again

The device keeps the tracks it was given, so the music can be carried on from the Spotify app while this app is closed, asleep or not reading the device. The player then works out where the queue has got to, without sending anything to the device:

- **The device still holds the tracks.** It is followed from the track it has reached, and the queue and source continue from there. This is the check described under rejoining, and it is also made by a page that stayed open: after 90 seconds without a read of the device, which is what a computer that slept looks like, and when a device that had stopped reporting is reported again.
- **The device stopped reporting.** Spotify forgets a paused or closed device after a while. The item is kept, shown paused where it was last seen, together with the tracks the device was given. When Spotify reports that device playing again, or paused on a later one of those tracks or on other music, it is picked up as above. Until then **Play** starts the kept item where it stopped.
- **The device holds something else, or nothing.** The player reads the account's play history once, `GET /v1/me/player/recently-played` with the time the session was last saved, and takes the tracks of the queue that Spotify played since, in order, as played. The first track not played becomes the current one, shown from its beginning, or stays due next when other music is shown. A list the device played to its end needs no history: the queue goes on after it.

The play history needs the `user-read-recently-played` permission. When Spotify refuses the read, it is not asked again until the page is reloaded, and the saved track is kept. The history holds at most the last 50 tracks and no podcast episodes. Tracks are matched to the queue by URI, then by title, because Spotify may have played another recording.

### Sources

A loaded Track can start while Browse is counting. The queue reads more matching tracks in view order while the count is unavailable. Shuffle across the full result and saving the source to the shared session wait until the count or the final page establishes the total. The source belongs to the shared player, so leaving its page does not dispose it. The source captures the view's query and ordering when playback starts; stored library pages and album tracks are loaded as needed for playback and the upcoming list. This reads existing metadata and does not trigger scraping or playlist imports.

A shuffled track list picks tracks spread over the whole list, so it does not read a page for each. The player draws every track it still wants ahead (up to 30) from different rows and reads them in one request, `GET /api/v1/tracks/positions`, which takes the view's query and a comma-separated `positions` list of at most 50 zero-based positions and returns `{ items: [{ position, item }] }`. Rows without a Spotify recording are dropped and another round is drawn for the shortfall; tracks already read are shown as upcoming while that round is read. A list played in order, a playlist and the album library still read pages.

## Failures

- **Spotify disconnected:** the token endpoint or Spotify rejected the account. The dock offers **Reconnect**, which repeats the playback consent.
- **A start the device ignores:** Spotify can accept a request to play and then start the track the device already held from its beginning. That track stays shown as what is playing and **Playback did not start** is reported; the requested item is not shown over other music, and the request is not sent again until it is asked for again. The start is judged by what begins on the device after the request:
  - The track this page last saw the device play, which a page that takes the session over reads as it does so, is the earlier playback.
  - For an album, a track outside the album's context is not the album. This needs no earlier look at the device, so it also holds on a page opened while the device was idle.
  - For a track, any other track that begins could be another recording of it, so the player asks Spotify once which recording it plays for the requested track in the account's market (`GET /v1/tracks/{id}?market=from_token`). The track that began is the requested one when it has that URI or the same name. Otherwise the start was ignored, also when the track is one of those sent after the requested one.
  - When Spotify plays no recording of the requested track there, or cannot be asked, the track that began is accepted, which is how the next track sent starts after one that cannot be played.
- **Other errors** (Premium required, device unavailable, rate limited, playback did not start) appear above the dock with a Spotify icon. The queue is kept.
- A command is not retried, except on this browser's own device: Spotify can answer that the device does not exist for a few seconds after the browser registers, so a start or a move to it is tried again every 700 ms for about four seconds before **That device is not available** is reported. Three state reads in a row that fail, or that report no playback, stop following the device: an item that had not finished is kept, shown paused where it was last seen, and **Play** starts it there. A failed background check for playback elsewhere is not reported; the next check repeats it.

## Limits

- The session is shared by asking the server, not pushed: another copy shows a change within about three seconds while something plays and eight while nothing does, and a hidden copy when it is shown again.
- When every page is closed, a Spotify device can play the queue and source tracks it has received. A Sonos room can play up to ten tracks loaded ahead. The next page opened checks where the device has reached and can use Spotify’s play history to recover the remaining queue.
- Rejoining decides from what Spotify reports: the playing track, its context and position. The Web API does not say whether the device's tracks were replaced by the same ones, so a queue started again elsewhere with the same tracks, further along than the saved track, is taken for the saved one. Moving back in the queue on the device (Previous, or seeking back more than five seconds in the saved track) counts as other music.
- For Spotify playback, the previous copy can still act for up to three seconds after another takes the queue over; longer for a background tab the browser has slowed down. A track due at that moment could be started twice.
- An album start that the device answers with a track of that same album, begun from its start, is taken for the album unless this page had seen the device on that track. Spotify also starts an album at another track when shuffle is on in the device, so the track alone does not tell.
- A start is judged by a track beginning from its start. A device that answers by carrying on in the middle of what it held is reported with **Playback did not start** after ten seconds.
- Every browser registers a Spotify device named **Media Manager**, so another browser's player appears under that name in **Play on**. A copy that has not played or been chosen as **This browser** is not registered yet and is not listed in the others; choose **This browser** in that copy instead.
- The queue goes on by itself only when its item was seen to end. An item that ends while no page reads the device for 90 seconds or more, or a list whose last track gives way to other music more than ten seconds after the last read, is not followed by the next one until **Play** or **Next**.
- The play history lists what Spotify counted as played; which tracks it lists and how soon is Spotify's decision, and a track missing from it stays in the queue. A title that occurs twice among the tracks given can be matched to the earlier one. It is not read for an album played through Spotify's album context.
- A saved session is kept until it is replaced; there is no expiry.
- No repeat control.
- When an album has no stored playable tracks, it plays through Spotify's album context instead; the manual queue resumes after that album and individual upcoming songs cannot be shown.
- Playback started elsewhere is shown as Spotify reports it. The app stores catalog summaries from current playback and eligible queue and recovery results. It leaves device details and play times in the playback session and uses the app queue for what is shown next. A change made on another device shows within three to five seconds.
- Spotify allows additions to the end of its manual queue. For a reorder, removal, queued album or repeated track that Spotify cannot stage in the requested position, the app saves the edit. The same applies if Spotify’s queue cannot be read. An open page corrects the order when the device changes tracks. In those cases the displaced track can be audible until the next device check.
- When another device jumps straight to the end of an album between two state reads, the album can go unnoticed as finished and the next queue entry does not start.
- Tests use a fake SDK player and fake Spotify responses. Audio, the consent round trip and phone browsers are not covered by automated tests.
