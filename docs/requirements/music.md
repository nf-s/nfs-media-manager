# Albums, music and playlists

## Album metadata

Preserve title, artists, artwork, duration, release/added dates, genres, countries, playlist memberships, provider IDs and links. Latest cleaned album title/art/artist selection is authoritative for initial browse parity; a different release returned by another service must not silently replace it.

Retain, display, filter and sort scrobbles; RYM rating/votes; Discogs rating/votes/popularity; Metacritic rating/votes; Spotify popularity; and Last.fm listeners. Preserve raw rating units, distinguish milliseconds/seconds correctly, and use UTC dates. Zero is a valid value. Default browse order is added date descending.

Spotify audio features are intentionally discontinued. Remove existing acousticness, danceability, energy, instrumentalness, liveness, loudness, musical mode/key, speechiness, tempo, valence, time signatures, analysis URLs and audio-feature containers from active storage, not just UI. Original source JSON and private restore backups remain unchanged. This is the sole targeted exception to raw-provider preservation below. Do not delete unrelated configuration/filter `mode` values, ratings, popularity, duration or track identity. See [cleanup and audit procedure](../spotify-audio-feature-removal.md).

## Requirements

- MUSIC-01: Preserve every current cleaned album and all raw provider snapshots; match by provider references to canonical IDs. Keep non-Spotify provider references and support future services without changing IDs.
- MUSIC-02: A clearly labelled Spotify outbound link is visible on cards, table/detail views when available. Other service links include MusicBrainz, Discogs, Last.fm, RYM/Sonemic and Metacritic. Do not fabricate broken URLs for missing IDs.
- MUSIC-02a: Album genres list Rate Your Music genres first, then other tags alphabetically, then year and decade tags such as "2026" or "90s". Only tags recorded with a Rate Your Music source are recognised as such.
- MUSIC-03: Overview shows art, title, artists, dates, duration, useful ratings and clickable genres/countries/playlists. Tracks are a separate tab, not an unexplained wall of default content. Preserve disc/track order, artists, duration and external IDs.
- MUSIC-04: Preserve artist metadata used to derive genres/countries, blacklist/missing-match information, playlist memberships and playlist definitions. Playlists lists imported playlists with their track and album counts; a playlist opens in the playlist drawer (MUSIC-11c), which can apply it as a filter on Tracks.
- MUSIC-05: Smart playlist definitions retain name, filter modes/ranges, sort, limit and provider destination. Preview locally. Publishing a smart playlist to Spotify is out of scope; reading playlists is covered by MUSIC-11 and adding an album to one by MUSIC-11b.
- MUSIC-06: Outbound links are the default way to listen. Connecting a Spotify account is optional and explicit, and grants read-only saved-album discovery, playlist listing and playlist import. Adding an album to a playlist (MUSIC-11b) needs a separate, explicit permission to change playlists; without it everything else keeps working. In-app playback is a separate, explicit connection in Providers: it plays and queues library albums and tracks in the browser or on another Spotify device, shows and controls what the account is already playing on another device, keeps its queue in the open page, sends the browser a short-lived access token only, and never changes a playlist or the Spotify library. With playback off, no play controls appear and no playback requests are made. See [Spotify playback](../spotify-playback.md).
- MUSIC-07: Audio-feature cleanup is explicit, backed up, auditable and idempotent. Remove retired feature values from raw snapshots, current provider state, normalized metadata, tracks, overrides, stored job payloads and saved-view filters/sorts. Keep history rows, timestamps and references. Legacy imports and provider persistence must not reintroduce them; no audio-feature fetches or browse fields remain supported.

- MUSIC-08: Album refreshes preserve canonical identity and distinguish release-specific observations from historical aggregates. Indexed ratings must match the underlying work, retain source and scope, and link to the validated page that supplied the score when available. An identity match alone is not a rating. Show optional edition, credit and track details from retained responses without fetching or overwriting edits. See [album provider contracts](../album-providers.md).
- MUSIC-09: Spotify metadata jobs use provider-owned references or an unambiguous match. Preserve known UPC and popularity when omitted; do not replace tracks from a partial response. Account discovery defaults to a bounded preview, not necessarily the newest albums library-wide. Only an explicitly confirmed complete scan may claim the newest across the library, ordered by saved date. See [Spotify albums and CSV imports](../spotify-album-imports.md).
- MUSIC-10: An optional Last.fm sync stores every scrobble of the configured account as its own record with a UTC timestamp. It runs as background Last.fm jobs visible in Jobs, needs no open page, is resumable, never retries a failure automatically, shares the Last.fm dispatcher and request gate and stays at or below one request per second. The first sync is started explicitly; later syncs follow an owner-set schedule that can be turned off. Scrobbles link to library albums by title and credited artist; ambiguous or unmatched scrobbles stay stored and unlinked, and every scrobbled artist and album name is listed with its linked album or the reason it is unlinked. Albums show listening over time for the album and each track, and browse can filter and sort by last listened date and by scrobbles in the last year and last five years. See [Last.fm scrobble history](../lastfm-scrobbles.md).
- MUSIC-11: Tracks are a separate library from albums, selected beside Albums and Films. A track is in it only through an explicitly imported Spotify playlist or Liked Songs; an album's track list does not add tracks. Imports are Spotify jobs visible in Jobs with their page progress; they only read from Spotify, replace the playlist's tracks, remove tracks left in no playlist, and never request a playlist the account neither owns nor collaborates on. Each imported playlist and Liked Songs can be re-imported on its own owner-set schedule, off by default. Tracks filter by playlist, including the intersection of several. A track links to its library album and shows that album's tags, labelled as the album's; an album counts as in a playlist when one of its linked tracks is, in filters, counts and details. Stored scrobbles link to tracks by artist and title. See [tracks and Spotify playlists](../tracks-and-playlists.md).
- MUSIC-11a: The account's playlist list is stored. Opening Imports → Spotify playlists shows the stored list with when it was listed, its import state, schedule and last check per playlist, and makes no Spotify request. Listing again is an explicit Spotify job visible in Jobs with its page progress; only one runs at a time, a failure keeps the stored list, and the list is never re-read automatically. All playlists appear in the shared table; those that cannot be imported say why.
- MUSIC-11b: An album's details can add the album to an imported Spotify playlist the account owns or collaborates on; Liked Songs, legacy playlists and others' playlists are not offered. Each addition is a Spotify job visible in Jobs that appends only the album's tracks missing from the playlist, never removes or reorders, and then re-imports the playlist. Refusals, such as an album without a Spotify reference, are shown as the server returns them. See [adding an album to a playlist](../tracks-and-playlists.md#adding-an-album-to-a-playlist).
- MUSIC-11c: A playlist opens in a drawer from the Playlists page and from any playlist shown in Tracks or a track's details, kept in the URL (`playlist`, and `tab=sync` for the Sync tab) so back, forward, reload and links reopen it. Its Tracks tab lists the entries in playlist order in the shared table, loading further pages as it scrolls; its header can filter Tracks by the playlist and opens it in Spotify. Its Sync tab sets the playlist's schedule, imports it now, and shows the last check, when the contents were stored, the next scheduled import or why there is none, and the latest import job with its page progress, error and a link to Jobs. A legacy playlist says it is not synced. The Playlists table shows each playlist's schedule and last check. See [tracks and Spotify playlists](../tracks-and-playlists.md#playlist-drawer).
- MUSIC-11d: With playback connected, every playable track in the Tracks table, the track drawer and the playlist drawer can be played on its own and added to the queue, with the album play button's style and states. A track without a Spotify track ID, or with playback off, has no play controls. See [Spotify playback](../spotify-playback.md).
- MUSIC-11: Album Added displays, sorting and date ranges use the earlier of the stored library date and first linked Last.fm scrobble. A first scrobble supplies an unknown stored date. Keep the original stored date editable and preserve all provider history; derive the effective date from current links so unlinking or relinking updates it without a destructive backfill.

Album browse also supports custom listening rules over rolling periods, date ranges and all stored history, combined with all/any matching and a separate configurable period for count sorting. See SEARCH-07 in [search requirements](search.md).

## Acceptance

Reconcile every album in the migration fixture against its cleaned input, excluding only retired audio features while preserving every other nonempty metric, dates and links. Test an album with zero values, one with many genres, one missing artwork, one with Spotify data and one with playlist membership. Verify raw snapshots and track lists match input apart from this targeted removal without making Spotify requests. A cleanup dry run after apply must report zero changed rows; retained backup copies must contain the original removed values.
