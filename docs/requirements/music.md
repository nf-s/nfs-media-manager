# Albums, music and playlists

## Album metadata

Preserve title, artists, artwork, duration, release/added dates, genres, countries, playlist memberships, provider IDs and links. Latest cleaned album title/art/artist selection is authoritative for initial browse parity; a different release returned by another service must not silently replace it.

Retain, display, filter and sort scrobbles; RYM rating/votes; Discogs rating/votes/popularity; Metacritic rating/votes; Spotify popularity; and Last.fm listeners. Preserve raw rating units, distinguish milliseconds/seconds correctly, and use UTC dates. Zero is a valid value. Default browse order is added date descending.

Spotify audio features are intentionally discontinued. Remove existing acousticness, danceability, energy, instrumentalness, liveness, loudness, musical mode/key, speechiness, tempo, valence, time signatures, analysis URLs and audio-feature containers from active storage, not just UI. Original source JSON and private restore backups remain unchanged. This is the sole targeted exception to raw-provider preservation below. Do not delete unrelated configuration/filter `mode` values, ratings, popularity, duration or track identity. See [cleanup and audit procedure](../spotify-audio-feature-removal.md).

## Requirements

- MUSIC-01: Preserve every current cleaned album and all raw provider snapshots; match by provider references to canonical IDs. Keep non-Spotify provider references and support future services without changing IDs.
- MUSIC-02: A clearly labelled Spotify outbound link is at the end of the detail header when available; cards, rows and table cells carry no provider icons. Other service links include MusicBrainz, Discogs, Last.fm, RYM/Sonemic and Metacritic. Do not fabricate broken URLs for missing IDs.
- MUSIC-02a: Album genres list Rate Your Music genres first, then other tags alphabetically, then year and decade tags such as "2026" or "90s". Only tags recorded with a Rate Your Music source are recognised as such.
- MUSIC-03: Overview shows art, title, artists, dates, duration, useful ratings and clickable genres/countries/playlists. A headline rating opens the page that supplied its score, at every screen width; a provider whose rating is not among the headline figures keeps its link in the header. Tracks are a section of Overview after the headline figures, not an unexplained wall of default content. Preserve disc/track order, artists, duration and external IDs.
- MUSIC-04: Preserve artist metadata used to derive genres/countries, blacklist/missing-match information, playlist memberships and playlist definitions. Playlists lists imported playlists with their track and album counts; a playlist opens in the playlist drawer (MUSIC-11c), which can apply it as a filter on Tracks.
- MUSIC-05: Smart playlist definitions retain name, filter modes/ranges, sort, limit and provider destination. Preview locally. Publishing a smart playlist to Spotify is out of scope; reading playlists is covered by MUSIC-11 and adding an album to one by MUSIC-11b.
- MUSIC-06: Outbound links are the default way to listen. Connecting a Spotify account is optional and explicit, and grants read-only saved-album discovery, playlist listing and playlist import. Adding an album to a playlist (MUSIC-11b) needs a separate, explicit permission to change playlists; without it everything else keeps working. In-app playback is a separate, explicit connection made from the player: it plays and queues library albums and tracks in the browser or on another Spotify device, shows and controls what the account is already playing on another device, keeps its queue in the open page, sends the browser a short-lived access token only, and never changes a playlist or the Spotify library. With playback off, no play controls appear and no playback requests are made. See [Spotify playback](../spotify-playback.md).
- MUSIC-07: Cleanup of Spotify's audio features is explicit, backed up, auditable and idempotent. Remove the retired Spotify feature values from raw snapshots, current provider state, normalized metadata, tracks, overrides, stored job payloads and saved-view filters/sorts. Keep history rows, timestamps and references. Legacy imports and provider persistence must not reintroduce them; no Spotify audio-feature fetches or browse fields remain supported. Tempo and key reported by another provider are separate track facts with their own source, not Spotify's audio features: see [SONO-13 to SONO-17](sonovault.md#stage-2-tempo-key-and-genre-on-tracks).

- MUSIC-08: Album refreshes preserve canonical identity and distinguish release-specific observations from historical aggregates. Indexed ratings must match the underlying work, retain source and scope, and link to the validated page that supplied the score when available. An identity match alone is not a rating. Show optional edition, credit and track details from retained responses without fetching or overwriting edits. See [album provider contracts](../album-providers.md).
- MUSIC-09: Spotify metadata jobs use provider-owned references or an unambiguous match. Preserve known UPC and popularity when omitted; do not replace tracks from a partial response. Account discovery defaults to a bounded preview, not necessarily the newest albums library-wide. Only an explicitly confirmed complete scan may claim the newest across the library, ordered by saved date. See [Spotify albums and CSV imports](../spotify-album-imports.md).
- MUSIC-10: An optional Last.fm sync stores every scrobble of the configured account as its own record with a UTC timestamp. It runs as background Last.fm jobs visible in Jobs, needs no open page, is resumable, never retries a failure automatically, shares the Last.fm dispatcher and request gate and stays at or below one request per second. The first sync is started explicitly; later syncs follow an owner-set schedule that can be turned off. Scrobbles link to library albums by title and credited artist; ambiguous or unmatched scrobbles stay stored and unlinked, and every scrobbled artist and album name is listed with its linked album or the reason it is unlinked. Albums, tracks, artists and playlists show listening over time in the shared trend chart (albums also per track), and browse can filter and sort by last listened date and by scrobbles in the last year and last five years. See [Last.fm scrobble history](../lastfm-scrobbles.md).
- MUSIC-11: Tracks are a separate library from albums, selected beside Albums and Films. Every stored album track position references a track in the library. Importing a Spotify playlist, Liked Songs or stored Last.fm history can contribute membership to the same recording. Imports are Spotify jobs visible in Jobs with their page progress; they only read from Spotify, replace the playlist's tracks, remove tracks with no remaining album, playlist or Last.fm membership, and never request a playlist the account neither owns nor collaborates on. Each imported playlist and Liked Songs can be re-imported on its own owner-set schedule, off by default. Tracks filter by playlist, including the intersection of several. A track links to its library album and shows that album's tags, labelled as the album's; an album counts as in a playlist when one of its linked tracks is, in filters, counts and details. Stored scrobbles link to tracks by artist and title. See [tracks and Spotify playlists](../tracks-and-playlists.md).
- MUSIC-11a: The account's playlist list is stored. Opening Imports → Spotify playlists shows the stored list with when it was listed, its import state, schedule and last check per playlist, and makes no Spotify request. Listing again is an explicit Spotify job visible in Jobs with its page progress; only one runs at a time, a failure keeps the stored list, and the list is never re-read automatically. All playlists appear in the shared table; those that cannot be imported say why.
- MUSIC-11b: An album's details can add the album to an imported Spotify playlist the account owns or collaborates on; Liked Songs, legacy playlists and others' playlists are not offered. Each addition is a Spotify job visible in Jobs that appends only the album's tracks missing from the playlist, never removes or reorders, and then re-imports the playlist. Refusals, such as an album without a Spotify reference, are shown as the server returns them. See [adding an album to a playlist](../tracks-and-playlists.md#adding-an-album-to-a-playlist).
- MUSIC-11c: A playlist opens in a drawer from the Playlists page, Tracks or a track's details. Its URL restores the open detail and tab. Overview automatically groups playlists made up largely of whole or nearly whole releases into distinct library albums, ordered by first appearance. Track mixes and Liked Songs open in track order. Playlists with both kinds of contents offer an Albums/Tracks switch. Both views use bounded server pages and preserve their settings in the detail URL. The header can filter Tracks by playlist and open Spotify. Sources shows the stored source and import date. For synced playlists, it offers the schedule and **Sync now** and shows the latest check, next run and import job with progress and errors. The Playlists table shows the schedule and last check. See [tracks and Spotify playlists](../tracks-and-playlists.md#playlist-drawer).
- MUSIC-11d: With playback connected, every playable track in the Tracks table, the track drawer and the playlist drawer can be played and added to the queue, with the album play button's style and states. Playing from a browse or playlist view retains its filters and order as the playback source across navigation. Manually queued items play first after the current source song, then the source resumes. Shuffle changes only the source; the queue shows up to ten upcoming source tracks and links back to its view. A track without a Spotify track ID, or with playback off, has no play controls. See [Spotify playback](../spotify-playback.md).
- MUSIC-11e: **Add to Library** in a track's Overview or Sources adds its missing album by Spotify ID. Opening the album title uses its catalog or Spotify release identity and preserves library membership. An uncached release opens with a direct read of that exact Spotify album, including the track page it returns. Full enrichment is a separate action. A legacy match can recover the release from its Spotify track ID. An unmatched library track offers manual Spotify matching beside its title. Another Spotify release of the same title and artists keeps its own library entry. After a direct addition, the track shows its linked album or gives a link to the added album with an explanation. Sources also warns about each library album that counts plays of the track without listing it. Results for adding an album start with their track count, and a release with fewer tracks than were played under the scrobbled name says so and is listed last. See [track details](../tracks-and-playlists.md#track-details) and [adding from scrobbles](../lastfm-scrobbles.md).
- MUSIC-11: Album Added displays, sorting and date ranges use the earlier of the stored library date and first linked Last.fm scrobble. A first scrobble supplies an unknown stored date. Keep the original stored date editable and preserve all provider history; derive the effective date from current links so unlinking or relinking updates it without a destructive backfill.

Albums, Tracks and Artists Browse also support custom listening conditions over rolling periods, date ranges and all stored history, set on a histogram or as rules, combined with all/any matching and one configurable period for the Scrobbles column and sort. See SEARCH-07 in [search requirements](search.md).

- MUSIC-12: Artists have their own library dropdown entry with Browse, and artist metadata selection is Jobs → Metadata with the Artists library filter, while details remain shared by Albums and Tracks and list their library albums, tracks and playlists. Browse and metadata selection support searchable, multiple-playlist filters. Metadata lists each provider's successful scrape date separately from attempt status, with freshness filtering and explicit artist/provider selection before queueing. Artist enrichment preserves snapshot history and shares existing request limits; artist jobs remain separate from album jobs, including legacy history. Artist tags supplement fewer than two distinct visible album tags at read time; keep album and artist provenance distinct without copying memberships. Tags filters follow the displayed fallback, with separate Album tags and Artist tags filters available. See [artists, people and channels](../artists-and-people.md).

- MUSIC-13: Albums, tracks, artists and playlists share one listening series: daily counts for five years and monthly counts for all time, from stored, linked scrobbles. Every listening chart is the shared trend chart fed by this series: 30 days, 1 year, 5 years and all time, with the grain chosen from the data. All time, the default, and every listening sparkline start at the earliest stored scrobble of anything, not at the first scrobble of the item, so every chart covers the same period. Album Listening also offers one scrobbled track at a time, charted by month from the album listening report. See [Last.fm scrobble history](../lastfm-scrobbles.md#in-the-app).
- MUSIC-14: The playlist Coverage tab shows how many library albums have stored metadata from any source and, for each provider, how many eligible albums have its metadata, lack it or have stale results. Provider data from an import counts alongside scraped data. Artist album coverage shows scrape freshness. Both return up to 100 album IDs per gap and use 180 days as the default refresh interval. A playlist's albums are its album entries and the library albums of its tracks; an artist's are the albums credited to it. Reading coverage never scrapes or queues. Queueing the gap uses the reviewed album queue (`POST /api/v1/scrape/jobs/albums`) with its limits, broad-run confirmation, freshness recheck and active-job checks. The Coverage tab shows one row per provider (covered of eligible, with **Queue N missing** and **Refresh N stale**, or the reason it cannot queue: credentials required, provider halted). On a playlist the counts are links (**N missing metadata**, **N stale**) beside **Queue** and **Refresh**. A count opens the affected library albums or track entries in the current contents view. **Metadata gaps** above the entries does the same for any provider, with a list to pick the gap, and is kept in the URL (`playlistGaps`). The filtered rows (`GET /api/v1/playlists/:id/coverage/entries`, read-only) name the providers each entry lacks; selecting rows offers the provider that fills them (chosen from a list when several do) and the same review. Either action opens a review that names the provider, the number of jobs (at most 100 albums per request, saying when more are missing) and that skipped albums are expected; runs above five jobs are flagged and sent with broad-run confirmation only after **Confirm N metadata jobs**. The result reports queued, skipped and warnings, links to the batch, and coverage is reloaded. A playlist's Coverage tab also counts its artists (**Coverage of**: Albums or Artists, kept in the URL as `coverage=artists`): the artists credited on its tracks, on its album entries and on the albums of its tracks (`GET /api/v1/playlists/:id/coverage/artists`, read-only), per artist provider, as current, stale or never fetched. The artist counts link to **Jobs → Metadata** for Artists filtered to the playlist and that provider's freshness, and **Queue** and **Refresh** queue up to 100 of those artists through `POST /api/v1/artists/enrichment` after the same review. Queueing artists never queues an album, so a playlist can be kept current for artists alone.

- MUSIC-15: Every change to tracks, albums and artists, made by the owner or by an automatic rule, is recorded. Apart from the recordings an album track-list refresh removes, each can be undone and made again, including everything that followed it and a track the change removed, which returns with its credits, tags, provider data, links and edits. Undo is offered only while the parts are still where the change put them, and a change that cannot be undone says why. Tracks can be merged from a selection in the Tracks table, an artist's Tracks list and an album's track list; the merge is made at once unless a version word, the lengths, the artists, two positions on one album, a kept-separate decision or names that would follow elsewhere speak against it, and then a review opens first. The server refuses an unreviewed merge with a caution. Tracks, Albums and Artists each have a History, and a record's Sources lists its own changes. See [merging and history](../tracks-and-playlists.md#merging-and-history).
- MUSIC-15a: A merge deletes no Spotify track or album. Tracks with their own Spotify track become stored versions of one record that share its scrobbles; a Last.fm-only track gives its names, album positions and playlist entries to the kept track. A track's Sources shows what it is made of, with Shown, Separate and Merge with…. See [versions](../tracks-and-playlists.md#versions).
- MUSIC-15b: Tracks → Matches offers Possible duplicates: a grouped view of the table with Merge and Keep separate on each group, what Merge does and the evidence behind an icon, what speaks against merging on the row, a filter for groups with or without something against them, and a notice on the track page. Rows can be ticked and merged from the bar; a group's Merge merges only that group's ticked rows, or the whole group when none is ticked. Merge and Keep separate are made in the background, in the order pressed. Keep separate is stored; the group is not suggested again and automatic matching never joins its tracks. See [possible duplicates](../tracks-and-playlists.md#possible-duplicates-and-keep-separate).
- MUSIC-15c: Albums merged from the Albums table become editions of the kept album, whose scrobbles count every edition; an edition can be separated again. A scrobbled album name moved onto an album brings the track names scrobbled under it that match the album's tracks, and a track merge brings the album names its titles were scrobbled under that are linked to no album. See [albums and editions](../tracks-and-playlists.md#albums-and-editions).
- MUSIC-15d: Artists merged from the Artists table keep the other names, and a merged artist's page and links open the kept artist. An artist with film credits cannot be merged. See [merging artists](../artists-and-people.md#merging-artists).
- MUSIC-15e: Automatic matches from imports, Spotify track searches and album track lists are listed in History with their rule. An undone automatic match, and a name separated from a track a rule gave it, is not matched there again until the owner returns the name to automatic matching. See [automatic matches in History](../tracks-and-playlists.md#automatic-matches-in-history).

## Acceptance

Reconcile every album in the migration fixture against its cleaned input, excluding only retired audio features while preserving every other nonempty metric, dates and links. Test an album with zero values, one with many genres, one missing artwork, one with Spotify data and one with playlist membership. Verify raw snapshots and track lists match input apart from this targeted removal without making Spotify requests. A cleanup dry run after apply must report zero changed rows; retained backup copies must contain the original removed values.
